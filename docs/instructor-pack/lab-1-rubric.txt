Lab 1 Rubric
OpsMail Assistant: Indirect Prompt Injection via Poisoned Inbox
Total: 100 points

Difficulty: Medium

Assessment Type: Security Lab Report

Rubric Purpose
This rubric is designed to grade Lab 1 submissions using objective, evidence-based criteria.
Students are assessed on exploit completion, trace-backed evidence, conceptual understanding, root-cause analysis, mitigation quality, and professional reporting.

1. Assessment Overview
Students are expected to exploit an indirect prompt injection vulnerability in the OpsMail Assistant
lab environment and submit a short security report.
A high-quality submission should demonstrate that the student can:
1. identify the attacker-controlled input source;
2. inject a malicious email payload;
3. induce the assistant to process that email;
4. capture trace-backed evidence of the exploit path;
5. demonstrate disclosure of the protected target information;
6. explain the root cause of the failure;
7. propose realistic mitigations;
8. communicate the finding in a professional security report.

3. Constraint-Based Grading Model
This rubric uses a constraint-based model. Each criterion is expressed as a grading constraint:
• Relevance: Does the submission address the required concept or task?
• Satisfaction: Does the submission satisfy the criterion with adequate evidence or explanation?
• Feedback: What should the instructor tell the student if the criterion is not satisfied?
Where possible, criteria are binary or tiered. Award partial credit only where the rubric explicitly allows it.

7. Instructor Notes
7.1 Recommended Grading Approach
First verify whether the student achieved the core exploit objective. Then grade the report for
evidence, explanation, and mitigation quality.
A student who captures the flag but cannot explain the trust-boundary failure should not receive
a high distinction. Conversely, a student who narrowly misses the flag but provides strong trace
analysis and a correct explanation may still receive partial credit under the evidence, vulnerability,
and mitigation categories.

7.2 Common Weak Submissions
Common issues include:
• describing the exploit as ordinary social engineering rather than indirect prompt injection;
• including a payload but no trace evidence;
• showing the final answer but not the tool or email-read event;
• saying “the prompt worked” without explaining instruction/data confusion;
• recommending only “make the prompt stricter” without least privilege or output controls;
• failing to identify confidentiality as the impacted security property.

7.3 Minimum Passing Standard
A minimum passing submission should:
• use the email inbox as the attack vector;
• attempt to trigger the assistant through normal chat interaction;
• identify the vulnerability as indirect prompt injection;
• provide at least one piece of evidence from the lab;
• propose at least one plausible mitigation.
